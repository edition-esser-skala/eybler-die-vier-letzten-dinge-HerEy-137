Comprises “Recit: N|r|o 8.” (= no.~13; fol.~1r) and “Duetto” (= no.~14; fol.~1v–12r); fol. 12v is empty.

Folios are numbered in the upper right corner of each 5th and last recto page (pencil: 1–2656). A clean ink fair copy by unidentifed hand with very few changes, but several cancellations with red pencil.

\B2 is a full score comprising three volumes (22.5 × 30 cm, 98, 117, and 123 fol.) bound in blue-green cardboard. The cover label reads: “Die | 4 letzten Dinge. | 1|t|e|r [vol. 2: 2|t|er; vol. 3: 3|t|e|r] Theil.”. Hand-ruled paper, 12 staves per page (7.5 mm) in light brown ink and music in dark brown ink. Folios are numbered in the upper right corner of each recto page (brown ink: 1–338). In addition, folio numbers have been added by different hand in the upper right corner of ca. every 10th folio (pencil: 1, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40 …). A clean ink fair copy by unidentified hand with very few changes. Each volume comprises one \textit{Abtheilung}: vol. 1, fol. 1r: title (“Die 4 letzten Dinge. | Erſte Abtheilung.”); 1v–98v: music; vol. 2, fol. 99r: title (“Die 4 letzten Dinge. | Zweite Abtheilung.”); 99v–215r: music; 215v; empty; vol. 3, fol. 216r: title (“Die 4 letzten Dinge. | Dritte Abtheilung.”); 216v–338r: music; 338v: empty. The thir volume is signed on fol. 307v by Eybler (“di Giuseppe Eybler [manu propria] 810.”)

\B3 is a vocal score (23 × 33 cm, 105 fol.) bound in black-pink marbled cardboard. The title on fol. 1r reads: “Capo Chorista”. Hand-ruled paper, 12 staves per page (7 mm) in light brown ink and music in dark brown ink. Folios are numbered in the upper right corner of every 5th recto page (pencil: 1, 5, 10 …). A clean ink fair copy by unidentified hand with very few changes, but several cancellations with red pencil. Fol. 1r: title; 1v–104r: music; 104v–105v: empty.

\D1 consists of a full score (23.5 × 32 cm, 12 fol.) and 18 parts (same size) in modern binding. Hand-ruled paper, 10 staves per page (8 mm) in brown ink and music in black ink. A clean ink fair copy by two unidentified scribes, comprising the recitativo “Ihr Glücklichen” (no.~21) followed by the chorus “O Heil euch, Heil” (no.~22). Score (first scribe), fol. 1r: title (“Die vier letzten Dinge | Coro | /: O Heil euch Heil :/ | Vom H. Joſ. Eybler.”); 1v–12r: music; 12v: empty. Parts by first scribe: S 1 solo and coro (2 fol.), S 1 coro (1), S 2 coro (2x; 1, 1), A 1 coro (2x; 1, 1), A 2 coro (2x; 2, 2); parts by second scribe: S 1 coro (3x; 1, 1, 1), S 2 coro (3x; 1, 1, 1), A 1 coro (2x; 1, 1), and A 2 coro (2x; 1, 1).

\section{Erſte Abtheilung}

\begin{lyrics}{[1 L’Overtura]}
  \voice[]
  Die Ouverture charakteriſiert den Zuſtand der bangen Erwartung
  des allgemeinen Untergangs der Dinge – das Entſetzen bey dem
  Gedanken eines allgemeinen Endes – die Angſt vor dem Tode bey
  den Lebenden – die Seufzer der Sterbenden – dann den Kampf der
  Elemente – den Einſturz des Weltgebäudes.\\
  (\C1/\C2: Die bange Erwartung des allgemeinen Untergangs
  der Dinge.)
\end{lyrics}

\begin{lyrics}{[2] Chor der Engel}
  \voice[]
  Groß iſt der Herr – Er ſchuf die Welt,\\
  Und ſchloß in Zeit und Raum ſie ein;\\
  Bald winket er – und ſie zerfällt,\\
  Und was da war – wird nicht mehr ſeyn!
\end{lyrics}

\begin{lyrics}{[3] Rezitativ}
  \voice[Erſter Erzengel]
  Am Ziele ſchweben die erſchaffnen Dinge,\\
  Und aus dem endeloſen Ringe\\
  Des Alls wird dieſer Schöpfung Glied gelöſt.\\
  Das Leben, das in Staub verweſt,\\
  War nicht der Menſchen Ziel. Hervor, in neuem Lichte,\\
  Wird, was als Aſche ſchlummert, gehn,\\
  Und vor dem ſchrecklichen Gerichte,\\
  Lohn oder Straf’ erwartend ſtehn.\\
  Was unvergänglich ſchien, wird, wenn [\A1: wann] er winkt, vergehn,\\
  Und alles, dem Jehovahs großes Werde\\
  Das Daſeyn und das Leben gab,\\
  Die Sonne, Mond, Geſtirn’, und Erde\\
  Umſchließt des Tods und der Vernichtung Grab.
\end{lyrics}
